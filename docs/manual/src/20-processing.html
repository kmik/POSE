6Images and cameras

6.1 Images and metadata

POSE reads JPEG, PNG, TIFF (including BigTIFF and 16-bit TIFF, decoded to 8 bits) and BMP; -f sets the extensions. Images are processed in filename order. Large images are read in windows, so their size is limited by disk, not memory.

From the metadata POSE uses:

  • GPS position (EXIF latitude, longitude and altitude) for pair selection and georeferencing.
  • Camera make, model, serial number and focal length to group images into cameras and to start the calibration.
  • DJI XMP data where present: calibrated focal length, flight speed (for GPS time offset and rolling shutter), height above take-off and the RTK status.
Image rotation. POSE uses the pixels as stored and ignores the EXIF display orientation. Do not rotate or crop the photographs before processing, and use calibrations and orientations that refer to the unrotated images.

The originals are never modified. Their paths and checksums are recorded in each run; --copy-inputs also archives the originals in the run.

6.2 Processing resolution

Images are processed at a reduced size unless you ask for full resolution:

--qualityImage sizeUse
ultraoriginalFinest detail; slowest, most memory and disk.
high1/2 of each sideNormal production setting.
medium1/4Quick results, tests.
low1/8Previews.
custom (default)longest side at most --max-dimension (1600 px)Command-line default.

An explicit --max-dimension also caps a named quality. Alignment can use a different resolution from dense matching: --alignment-quality or --alignment-max-dimension. Images are never enlarged.

--estimate-memory prints the resolution and memory plan for the chosen settings and exits; the plan is also saved in every run (00_inputs/resolution_plan.json).

6.3 Cameras and camera groups

Images taken with the same camera and lens share one calibration. By default (--camera-grouping auto) POSE groups the images by the camera IDs of an imported orientation, else by --camera-groups FILE, else by EXIF make, model, serial number and focal length. single puts every image into one camera, per-image gives each image its own. Images of different pixel sizes are always separate cameras.

The cameras are named camera_1, camera_2, ... in the order of each camera's first image, or by your names in the --camera-groups file (CSV image,camera). They are listed with make, model and focal length in 01_calibration/cameras.json. Options that take CAMERA=VALUE apply to one camera:

photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --camera-groups cams.csv \
  --calibration rgb=rgb.yaml --calibration nir=nir_camera.txt --intrinsics-mode nir=prior

6.4 Calibration

Automatic calibration (default)

Without a supplied calibration, the focal length starts from the EXIF or DJI XMP focal length (--quick-calibration, on by default). The lens is then refined during alignment, one parameter at a time (f, k1, k2, cx, cy, p1, p2, k3). A parameter is kept only when the data support it and two held-out groups of tie points improve. Without a usable EXIF focal length, or with --quick-calibration off, POSE first estimates focal length and radial distortion from image pairs (the bootstrap).

For large blocks the parameter trials are costly. --calibration-trial-method chooses how they are run:

MethodHowWhen
full (default)Each trial is a bundle adjustment of the whole block. Skipped above about 120 cameras per camera model, where no trial can be accepted.Small and medium blocks.
subsetTrials on about 100 cameras near the block centre (--calibration-trial-cameras), then one whole-block fit of the chosen model, kept only if it improves the whole block.Large blocks.
jointUnsupported parameters are dropped first; the rest are fitted together once.Large blocks, fastest.

--calibration-acceptance relaxed accepts a fit when neither held-out group gets worse and every parameter moved by at least three standard deviations; the default strict requires both groups to improve. After georeferencing, the GPS-weighted adjustment refits the lens again with the GPS positions as priors (Section 8.1).

Supplied calibration

--calibration FILE supplies a calibration in POSE's own format, the 14-number calib.result, OpenCV YAML/XML, Metashape, MicMac, or a one-camera COLMAP or INPHO file. The calibration refers to the original image size and is fixed by default. --intrinsics-mode prior refines it with uncertainty penalties, initial uses it only as a start, and CAMERA=free ignores it for one camera. A focal length in millimetres needs the sensor width: --focal MM --s_w MM. --refine-intrinsics limits which parameters may be refined, and --no-self-calibration turns lens refinement off.

Examples: calibration
# Reuse the calibration of an earlier project, refining it slightly
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --calibration old-project/camera.txt --intrinsics-mode prior

# Large block: lens trials on a subset of about 100 cameras
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --calibration-trial-method subset

# Two cameras (RGB and NIR) with their own calibrations
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --camera-groups cams.csv \
  --calibration rgb=rgb.yaml --calibration nir=nir.txt

The final calibration of each run is written to camera.txt at the project root and in 01_calibration/; it can be supplied to later projects with the same camera.

6.5 Importing orientations

Calibrations and camera poses from other software can be imported: --orientation-input FILE_OR_DIRECTORY for a combined source, or --calibration and --poses separately. Supported formats are POSE native, CSV (matrix, quaternion or omega/phi/kappa), COLMAP, Bundler, openMVG, OpenSfM, Metashape, MicMac, OpenCV and INPHO; the format is detected automatically or chosen with --orientation-input-format. Photographs and cameras are matched by filename.

Supplied poses are fixed by default. --extrinsics-mode prior refines them with position and rotation uncertainties (--position-sigma, --rotation-sigma); initial uses them only as a start. When the poses are in a projected map coordinate system, declare it with --orientation-crs EPSG:CODE: the coordinates are then used as they are, without a GPS fit (Section 8.6). Appendix B gives the CSV layouts; conventions of the other formats are in the orientation documentation.

Examples: importing orientations
# A COLMAP model, kept fixed
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --orientation-input colmap/sparse/0

# An INPHO project in ETRS-TM35FIN, poses refined within their uncertainty
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --orientation-input block.prj \
  --orientation-crs EPSG:3067 --extrinsics-mode prior --position-sigma 0.1 --rotation-sigma 0.01

# Calibration from OpenCV, poses as omega/phi/kappa CSV
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --calibration camera.yml --calibration-format opencv \
  --poses exteriors.csv --poses-format csv-opk

6.6 Chessboard calibration

--calib estimates a camera calibration from photographs of a chessboard instead of reconstructing a scene. Give the number of inner corners (--board-columns, --board-rows) and the square size (--square-size, e.g. 0.02 for 2 cm). The result is a camera.txt with diagnostics.

7Alignment

Alignment finds the position and orientation of every camera from tie points: the same feature found in several photographs. It covers the steps match, align and optionally cleanup.

7.1 Features and matching

POSE detects up to --features keypoints per image (default 8000; the presets use 20000) with SIFT (default) or ORB, AKAZE, KAZE or BRISK (--matching-algorithm). Descriptors are matched with a ratio test (--ratio, default 0.75; presets 0.85) and verified geometrically. --descriptor-matcher flann is much faster than brute force for SIFT on large sets.

Over uniform texture, such as forest canopy, the strongest keypoints can crowd into a few parts of the image. --feature-grid N (for example 16) spreads them over N cells along the longer side, which helps registration.

7.2 Choosing image pairs

Not every pair of photographs is matched. --pair-selection auto (default) uses the nearest GPS positions when every image has GPS spread over at least 20 m, and a visual search otherwise. Each image is matched with its --neighbors best candidates (default 3; presets 5). Other modes: overlap, gps, sequential (next images by filename) and all.

  • --gps-neighbors N also matches each image with its N nearest GPS neighbours, which guarantees that neighbours along a flight line are tried.
  • --min-forward-overlap, --min-side-overlap and --min-pair-overlap reject pairs whose estimated footprint overlap is too small (percent). The presets use a 50% forward overlap.

7.3 Camera registration and bundle adjustment

Alignment starts from a well-matched seed pair and adds cameras one at a time. Every time the registered set has grown by --registration-bundle-growth percent (default 15), a bundle adjustment refines all cameras and points, which limits drift on large blocks. At the end, a final bundle adjustment refines poses, points and the lens. Tie points with large errors are removed (--track-max-error, --cleanup-passes).

The result is in 04_geometry/: the final cameras (final/exterior.csv, orientations.json), the sparse cloud and the registration report registration.json, which gives the reason for every image that could not be registered.

7.4 When images do not align

Unregistered images are skipped and reported. The Registration... window on the Project tab shows how each image was registered or why it failed. Remedies, from simplest:

  1. --gps-neighbors 8 and --feature-grid 16: more and better-spread candidate pairs (branch from match).
  2. --match-repair on: images with few accepted pairs, or in a separate part of the match graph, are matched against more nearby images.
  3. --registration-recovery on: further models are grown from the unregistered images and merged with the main one through shared images or GPS; remaining images are retried with GPS-checked pose estimation.
  4. --registration-pose-check on (needs GPS): rejects cameras that land far from their GPS position or tilted far from the rest of the block, which catches weakly tied cameras that settle in a wrong pose.

The window offers Redo matching with repair... and Redo alignment with recovery... for these.

Example: redo matching and alignment of a run with the repair options
photogrammetry match align georeference dense finish -o PROJECT --run ID --branch \
  --gps-neighbors 8 --feature-grid 16 --match-repair on --registration-recovery on

7.5 Tie-point cleanup

Like "gradual selection" in other software, the optional cleanup step removes weak tie points and adjusts the block again before georeferencing. Weak means a large reprojection error, few images, or a small intersection angle (an uncertain depth).

--tie-point-cleanupEffect
off (default)No cleanup.
autoAutomatic limits: error above three times the median, fewer than 2 images, angle below 1°, at most 20% removed.
customYour limits: --tie-point-max-error, --tie-point-min-views, --tie-point-min-angle, --tie-point-max-removed.
pauseThe run waits; choose the limits with histograms and a 3D preview in the window, which then continues the run.

Points are removed worst first, and every camera keeps at least 15 points. The report is 04_geometry/tie_points/cleanup.json.

Example: clean the tie points of an existing run with other limits
photogrammetry cleanup georeference dense finish -o PROJECT --run ID --branch \
  --tie-point-cleanup custom --tie-point-max-error 1.0 --tie-point-min-angle 2

7.6 Camera screening

After alignment, cameras whose reprojection error or GPS residual is far above the rest of the block, or that were taken in turns, are flagged (--camera-screening report, default). Flags appear in the report, in the 3D view (red cameras) and in 04_geometry/camera_screening.json. --camera-screening exclude also leaves flagged cameras out of dense matching.

8Georeferencing and coordinate systems

Georeferencing places the aligned block in map coordinates. POSE has three sources of reference, which can be combined: the GPS of the images (automatic), ground control points, and an airborne lidar surface. Imported orientations that are already in map coordinates need no georeferencing.

8.1 GPS georeferencing

When at least three registered images have GPS and the positions are not all on one line, the georeference step fits a 3D similarity (scale, rotation, shift) from the camera centres to the GPS positions, rejecting outliers beyond --gps-threshold (default 10 m). A single straight flight line is handled too: the roll about the line is chosen so that the cameras look straight down.

Then a GPS-weighted bundle adjustment (--gps-bundle prior, default) refines the block with the GPS positions as weighted priors. This corrects block bending that a similarity cannot, and also estimates:

  • the lens again (--gps-intrinsics, with a focal-length prior from the DJI XMP or EXIF, --gps-focal-prior);
  • a GPS time offset (--gps-time-offset), the antenna lever arm (--gps-lever-arm) and the rolling-shutter readout time (--rolling-shutter), which need camera velocities from the DJI XMP or capture times.

Set the GPS accuracy with --gps-sigma: the default 2 m suits consumer GNSS; use about 0.05 m for RTK or PPK. With DJI barometric heights the height sigma is 0.2 m by default (--gps-height-sigma), which stops lens distortion from doming a flat block. --gps makes georeferencing required; --no-gps turns it off, leaving the result in a local frame.

8.2 Coordinate systems

Outputs are in the projected coordinate system --crs (default EPSG:3067, ETRS-TM35FIN), in metres, with x east and y north. Any projected EPSG system in metres can be used. The system is written into the LAS, the GeoTIFFs, the PLY header and coordinates.json.

A height system can be added after a plus sign: --crs EPSG:3067+3900 means ETRS-TM35FIN with N2000 heights. The vertical code is written into the LAS, the DSM and DTM GeoTIFFs, the PLY header and coordinates.json, so GIS software knows what the heights are. The label converts nothing; see the next section for converting heights.

8.3 Heights and vertical datums

A height means nothing without its reference surface. GNSS receivers measure heights above the ellipsoid, a mathematical surface; national height systems such as N2000 are heights above the geoid, roughly mean sea level. In Finland the geoid is about 17 to 20 m above the ellipsoid, so mixing the two shifts everything by that much.

What drones record

  • RTK or PPK fixed (DJI XMP RtkFlag 50, RtkStdHgt a few centimetres): DJI RTK drones write ellipsoidal heights. The heights are accurate to centimetres and only need a datum conversion.
  • No RTK (RtkFlag 0 or missing): the altitude is the GPS height of the take-off point plus the barometric height above it. It is nominally above sea level, but typically 5 to 50 m off. A datum conversion does not fix that: use ground control points or a lidar reference for absolute heights. Heights within the block (tree heights, relief) are still correct.

You can check the RTK status of a photograph with any EXIF tool, for example exiftool -XMP-drone-dji:all IMAGE.JPG.

Converting heights: --source-geoid and --target-geoid

Each option takes ellipsoid or a geoid grid file. --source-geoid names the datum of the camera heights, --target-geoid the datum the outputs should be in; a side that is not given is the ellipsoid. With neither option heights are used as they are. The camera heights are converted before georeferencing, so every output is in the target datum, and coordinates.json records the conversion and the height shift applied.

SituationOptions
DJI RTK drone in Finland, N2000 outputs--target-geoid FIN2005N00-grid --crs EPSG:3067+3900
Heights above EGM96, outputs on the ellipsoid--source-geoid EGM96-grid
Imported exteriors on ellipsoidal heights, N2000 outputs--orientation-crs EPSG:3067 --target-geoid FIN2005N00-grid --crs EPSG:3067+3900

Example: convert an existing run's heights to N2000 without redoing alignment
photogrammetry georeference dense ground finish -o PROJECT --run ID --branch \
  --target-geoid fin2005n00.gtx --crs EPSG:3067+3900

The camera heights are re-read from the photographs and converted, so only georeferencing and the later steps run again.

Grid files are NOAA/PROJ .gtx files, or text files with one latitude longitude N node per line on a regular grid (degrees, metres). The Finnish FIN2005N00 grid is published by the National Land Survey of Finland; EGM96 grids are available from the PROJ data repository. A camera outside the grid stops the run with a message.

When a height label is given with --crs but no conversion, POSE warns that the label is right only if the heights are already in that system.

GCP heights are used as given: they must already be in the datum you want the outputs in.

8.4 Ground control points

Ground control points (GCPs) are surveyed points visible in the photographs. Write them to a CSV file in the output coordinate system:

gcp_id,x,y,z,sx,sy,sz,type
G1,605551.06,6938380.02,89.55,0.02,0.02,0.03,control
G2,605553.10,6938371.44,88.10,,,,check

sx, sy, sz are standard deviations in metres (defaults --gcp-sigma 0.02 and --gcp-height-sigma 0.03). type is control (used in the adjustment) or check (only measured, an independent accuracy estimate). --gcp-check auto holds back about one point in five as check points without editing the file; the kept control points are the most widely spread ones.

Workflow:

photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --gcp gcps.csv      # stops with gcp 'waiting'
photogrammetry gcp-edit -o PROJECT                      # pin the points; Done continues the run

In the GCP marker editor, select a GCP on the left and click its exact position in one image on the right. The other images are then refined by patch matching (blue markers, with a correlation score); check and accept them with A, or Accept N refined. Images whose view of the point is blocked by canopy or roofs are listed as probably hidden. Keys: ←/→ previous/next image, ↑/↓ previous/next GCP, +/- zoom, F fit, R reject, U unpin. Markers are saved as you go to 04_geometry/gcp/markers.json.

The gcp step needs at least three control points pinned in two or more images each (one is enough when the block is already GPS-georeferenced). It moves the block onto the GCPs and adjusts it with the GCPs as weighted points. Residuals of control and check points are in 04_geometry/gcp/gcp_report.json and in the report.

8.5 Lidar reference

--lidar-reference FILE|FOLDER georeferences the block to airborne laser scanning (one LAS/LAZ file or a folder of tiles, for example national lidar) instead of ground control. After the GPS fit, the lidar step estimates the three shifts, the tilts and the heading (and with --lidar-scale on the scale) that put the tie points onto the lidar surface. Cameras and tie points are moved before dense matching, so all products are in the lidar's coordinate system and height datum.

  • The lidar must cover the block plus a margin (--lidar-margin, 10 m). The step checks this first; Check lidar coverage in the window checks it before processing.
  • Open ground and roofs count more than canopy (--lidar-canopy-weight, 0.1), since canopy may have grown or been felled since the lidar was flown. Changed areas are left out.
  • Parameters the terrain cannot determine, such as horizontal shifts over flat ground, stay at their GPS values and are reported.
  • The search range around the GPS solution is --lidar-search-shift (10 m) and --lidar-search-angle (3°).

The lidar's horizontal system must equal --crs; --lidar-crs names it when the files do not, and --lidar-vertical-datum names the height datum. The result, with standard deviations and difference maps, is in the report's Lidar Reference page and in the Lidar result window. A lidar reference corrects the block as a whole, not its bending.

8.6 Supplied exterior orientations

Exterior orientations imported with --orientation-crs EPSG:CODE are taken to be in that projected system and used without a GPS fit; the outputs stay in that system. With --source-geoid/--target-geoid, their heights are converted like GPS heights: each camera moves vertically by the datum difference at its position. Without --orientation-crs, imported poses are in a local frame and the GPS of the images is used for georeferencing as usual.

9Dense reconstruction

The dense step computes a depth for (almost) every pixel and fuses the depths of all cameras into one point cloud.

9.1 Dense methods

--dense-methodHow it worksNotes
sgm (command-line default)Semi-global matching on rectified image pairs, then fusion of the pair depth maps.Robust and well tested; one depth map per pair.
mvsgm (window default, presets)Multi-view plane-sweep SGM: one depth map per camera from several neighbouring images at once, checked against the neighbours' depth maps.Better on canopy and thin structures, fewer layered surfaces. Needs --fusion-mode image.
patchmatchExperimental PatchMatch stereo on the GPU.For testing.

9.2 Presets

--preset applies settings found best in a parameter study against reference data. It takes a platform, drone or aircraft, optionally with a scene, forest or city: for example --preset drone,forest. A scene alone implies drone. Options you give yourself always win over the preset, whatever their order.

Every preset sets SIFT features with FLANN matching (20000 features, ratio 0.85, 5 neighbours, 50% forward overlap), quick calibration, and the multi-view dense setup: mvsgm with image fusion, graph-average fusion, robust outliers, equiangular subpixel fit, the edge-aware depth outlier filter, mild filtering, step 1 and optimal neighbour selection. On top of that:

PresetAdds
drone2 views per point, 4° minimum angle, 8 neighbours, best 3, census window 5, 1 consistent view, sigma-based fusion tolerance.
aircraft3 views, 4°, 6 neighbours, best 3, census window 3, 2 consistent views. A tested large-format setup not yet compared against reference data.
forestStronger smoothing (--p1 8 --p2 48) and the reference outlier rule.
citySharper edges (--p1 2) and census window 3.

In the window, the Preset menu on the New run tab fills in the same values; the fields stay editable.

Examples: presets
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --preset drone,forest --quality high
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --preset drone,city --quality high --orthomosaic
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --preset aircraft --quality medium
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --preset drone,forest --min-views 3   # own value wins

9.4 Multi-view SGM in detail

Example: multi-view SGM without a preset
photogrammetry -i IMAGES -o PROJECT --quality high --dense-method mvsgm \
  --fusion-mode image --fusion-strategy graph-average --min-views 2 --min-angle 4 \
  --mvsgm-neighbors 8 --mvsgm-cameras-in-flight auto

For each camera, mvsgm picks up to --mvsgm-neighbors neighbouring cameras (default 6; presets 6–8) that share tie points at good triangulation angles. --mvsgm-optimal-neighbors favours neighbours that complement each other in coverage and baseline direction. Depth is searched over fronto-parallel planes; each pixel's cost is the mean of its --mvsgm-best-k best neighbour costs (default 3), so a neighbour that does not see the pixel does not spoil it. SGM smooths the depth map along 8 paths, coarse to fine. A depth is then kept only when at least --mvsgm-consistent-views neighbour depth maps agree with it within --mvsgm-consistency-labels steps.

Several cameras can be computed at once with --mvsgm-cameras-in-flight N|auto; the result is identical to computing them one by one. Checkpoints (--dense-checkpoint) make long dense steps resumable (Section 5.3). --export-disparity-images keeps each camera's depth map as an image for inspection.

9.5 Depth fusion and filtering

Fusion turns the depth maps into points that several cameras agree on. --fusion-mode chooses the method: reference (default for pair SGM) checks each pair's depths against the other cameras; image (required for mvsgm) fuses per camera in image space. In image fusion, --fusion-strategy decides how the cameras' copies of one surface become points:

  • owner (default): the camera with the finest footprint emits its depth; other copies are dropped.
  • graph-average (presets): all agreeing depths along the ray are averaged, weighted by their precision. Offsets between cameras average out without thinning.

Filters that remove false points are on by default: free-space violations (--fusion-free-space), isolated depth spikes (--fusion-spike-filter) and extreme depths (--fusion-depth-outliers). --fusion-outliers robust removes a point only when independent cameras contradict it. --thin-surfaces is a preset for thin flat surfaces such as roofs and boards. The fusion tolerances (--fusion-relative, --fusion-pixels, --fusion-consensus) are set by --filtering and can be overridden one by one; see Appendix A.

9.6 Pairwise SGM settings

For --dense-method sgm, the disparity range of each pair is estimated automatically from the tie points; --disparities and --min-disparity fix it. Smoothness is set by the penalties --p1 and --p2 (defaults 4 and 24); higher values give smoother surfaces and rounder edges. The matching cost (--matching-cost), census window (--similarity-window), left/right check (--lr-threshold), uniqueness (--uniqueness) and median filter options are described in Appendix A. --pairwise-search tsgm runs a faster coarse-to-fine search. --stereo-pairs-in-flight computes several pairs at once.
